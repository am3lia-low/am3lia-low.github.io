Agentic AI · Safety · Evaluation

Bojji — calibrated triage for citizen emailSolo

A triage agent for a tax authority's income-tax inbox. Emails are PII-scrubbed locally, classified by a fine-tuned MiniLM into 10 intents, calibrated, and matched to the relevant SOP — then either drafted with Gemini or sent to an officer with a clear reason. The point isn't drafting; it's knowing when not to draft. LangGraph makes the safety gate structural: escalated emails have no edge to the drafting node.

Adaptive Healing EngineTeam

A self-healing loop for web apps. Five LLM-driven browser personas — flow, pricing, contract, security, data integrity — click through a shopping app like real users and flag inconsistencies from what they see on screen, with no bug oracle. Hackathon · GenAI · 3D

3D-YWA — step into the story you're readingTeam

Turns ordinary prose into a persistent, explorable 3D setting. Each passage produces a patch to the world rather than a fresh scene, so the desk in chapter three is the same desk from chapter one — moved only because the story moved it. Objects appear only when the text supports them, contradictions are recorded instead of overwritten, and every object carries its provenance. I built the narrative engine that extracts places, objects and relations from the text, and wired it through the shared contract into the React Three Fiber renderer.

Project Myko — desktop cat companionTeam

A small, round, black cat that lives on your desktop. Mostly she just watches quietly — but when she notices you reading a PDF or replying to email, she shifts into a context copilot, offering a quick summary or draft reply. Two cognition loops, a seven-sprite state machine, persistent memory across sessions, and optional voice. Built in a 3-hour hackathon.

§ 04

About

I build AI systems that are useful, measurable, and honest about what they don't know.

Most of my recent work is agents and the scaffolding around them: routing that escalates instead of guessing, retrieval that cites its sources, browser personas that test software like real users, and evaluations — calibration, risk-coverage curves, human review — that tell you whether any of it actually works. I care a lot about the unglamorous parts: safety gates, reproducibility, and benchmarks you can trust.

But I also build for the joy of it. A desktop cat that summarises your PDFs. A quiz generator that runs on trending topics. A pipeline that turns books into video essays. Dashboards and sites I can ship in a weekend. The playful projects keep the rigorous ones honest.
